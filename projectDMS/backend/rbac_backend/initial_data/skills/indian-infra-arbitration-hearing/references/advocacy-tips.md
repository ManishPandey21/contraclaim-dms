# Practical Advocacy Tips for Indian Construction Tribunals

- Lead with the contemporaneous record; tribunals give high weight to letters, site diaries and programmes written at the time.
- Use the Contract’s own language when quoting clauses.
- When dealing with concurrent delay, present a clear concurrency matrix rather than lengthy narrative.
- Quantum slides should show the build-up, not just the final number.
- Anticipate the “why was notice late?” and “where is the critical path proof?” questions.
- Keep visual aids simple — tribunals dislike dense slides.
- Always have a precise prayer ready: “We respectfully request an award of X days EOT and INR Y under Claim Z.”
